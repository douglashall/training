# 10-Week 3 Beach Minimum Half Marathon Plan

**Race:** 3 Beach Minimum Half Marathon  
**Race date:** Saturday, September 26, 2026, at 7:30 a.m.  
**Training block:** Monday, July 20 through Saturday, September 26, 2026

The best use of these ten weeks is not a major mileage build. Your ultra training has preserved a substantial aerobic base; the priorities are rebuilding running economy at faster speeds, raising threshold, and becoming comfortable at half-marathon effort again. Mileage will stay close to your current range, peaking at about 60–62 miles.

The course is an out-and-back with rolling sections on Jerusalem Road and Atlantic Avenue. That makes rolling-road tempos and effort-based pacing important.

This plan assumes you are healthy, have maintained 50–60 miles consistently, and are not still recovering from a recent 100K. If you raced an ultra in the past four weeks, reduce the first two weeks by roughly 20% and keep all early speed controlled.

## Training intensity guide

Your historical PR paces were approximately:

- 5K: **5:34 per mile**
- Half marathon: **5:58 per mile**
- Marathon: **6:26 per mile**

Those are useful context, but they should not dictate your opening workouts after five years of ultra-focused training.

| Intensity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Recovery/easy** | RPE 2–3 out of 10. Full sentences, relaxed mechanics. Pace is irrelevant. |
| **Steady** | RPE 4–5. Clearly quicker than easy, but sustainable for a long time. It must not turn into another tempo. |
| **HMP** | Current target half-marathon effort, RPE 6–7. Controlled and rhythmical rather than strained. |
| **Threshold/T** | Current 50–60-minute race effort, RPE 7–8. Hard but even, with no late collapse. |
| **Fast track reps** | Current mile-to-5K effort depending on rep length. Fast and mechanically crisp, never sprinting. |

On the first Thursday, use the average pace of the final two 10-minute repetitions as your provisional threshold pace. Initially set HMP approximately **10–20 seconds per mile slower** than that pace. Summer heat and humidity may distort the number, so effort should override the watch.

The September 6 tune-up 10K will give a better target. A useful starting heuristic is 10K pace plus about 16–20 seconds per mile:

| Tune-up 10K | Starting HMP range | Approximate flat-course half |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 35:30 | 5:59–6:03/mi | 1:18:25–1:19:15 |
| 36:00 | 6:04–6:08/mi | 1:19:25–1:20:20 |
| 37:00 | 6:13–6:17/mi | 1:21:35–1:22:25 |
| 38:00 | 6:23–6:27/mi | 1:23:40–1:24:30 |

These are target-setting tools, not predictions. The course’s rollers and any coastal wind make even effort more valuable than forcing uniform splits.

## Workout conventions

All listed mileage includes warm-up and cool-down.

Before each Tuesday track session, run 2–3 easy miles, complete a few mobility drills, and run four relaxed 20-second strides. Use trainers or road flats during the opening weeks rather than suddenly returning to spikes.

For strides, accelerate smoothly for about 20 seconds and take at least 60–90 seconds of very easy recovery.

**Every Monday is complete rest from running.** Gentle walking or a few minutes of mobility is optional, but there is no required training.

## Week 1: July 20–26 — 50–52 miles

**Monday:** Complete rest.

**Tuesday, 8 miles:** 2 sets of 6 × 200 meters at relaxed current mile-to-3K effort. Jog 200 meters after each repetition and 400 meters between sets. Finish feeling that another set would have been possible.

**Wednesday, 9 miles:** Easy.

**Thursday, 10 miles:** 3 × 10 minutes at threshold effort with 2 minutes easy jogging. Keep the first repetition deliberately controlled. Record pace and heart rate for each.

**Friday, 7 miles:** Very easy recovery.

**Saturday, 6 miles:** Easy, followed by 6 × 20-second strides.

**Sunday, 12 miles:** Easy on gently rolling roads. No fast finish.

## Week 2: July 27–August 2 — 54–56 miles

**Wednesday, 9 miles:** Easy.

**Friday, 7 miles:** Recovery.

**Saturday, 6 miles:** Easy plus 6 strides.

## Week 3: August 3–9 — 57–59 miles

**Wednesday, 10 miles:** Easy.

**Friday, 7 miles:** Recovery.

## Week 5: August 17–23 — 58–60 miles

The training now becomes more half-marathon-specific.

**Monday:** Complete rest.

**Tuesday, 9 miles:** 2 sets of 5 × 400 meters at current 5K pace. Jog 200 meters after each and 400 meters between sets.

**Wednesday, 10 miles:** Easy.

**Sunday, 6 miles:** Very easy.

## Week 6: August 24–30 — 60–62 miles

This is the peak-volume week.

**Monday:** Complete rest.

**Tuesday, 10 miles:** 2 sets of 3 × 600 meters at current 5K pace, jogging 200 meters after each and 400 meters between sets. Then run 4 × 200 meters relaxed and fast with a full 200-meter jog.

**Wednesday, 10 miles:** Easy.

**Thursday, 13 miles:** 7-mile continuous progression. Begin approximately HMP plus 25–30 seconds per mile and gradually progress toward HMP. The final 1–2 miles may reach about 5 seconds faster than HMP only if you are still running smoothly. The average pace should remain slower than HMP.

**Friday, 7 miles:** Recovery.

**Saturday, 7 miles:** Easy, optionally with 6 strides.

**Sunday, 15 miles:** Easy on rolling roads. No fast finish after Thursday’s progression.

Run Thursday at or near 7:30 a.m. when practical, using your intended race breakfast and shoes.

## Week 7: August 31–September 6 — 49–51 miles

This is a tune-up and cutback week.

**Monday:** Complete rest.

**Tuesday, 8 miles:** 8 × 200 meters at relaxed current mile effort, with a full 200-meter jog after every repetition. This is a sharpening session, not a test.

**Wednesday, 9 miles:** Easy.

**Thursday, 9 miles:** 20–25 minutes continuously at HMP, followed by 4 relaxed strides. Finish knowing you could have continued.

**Friday, 6 miles:** Recovery.

**Saturday, 6 miles:** Easy with 4 brief strides.

**Sunday, 11–13 miles total:** Run a 10K race or solo time trial. Include approximately 3 miles of warm-up, drills and strides, the 10K hard but evenly paced, and 2–4 miles of very easy cool-down. Do not extend the cool-down simply to satisfy the mileage target if your legs are depleted.

Use the result to establish your goal HMP for the final three weeks.

## Week 10: September 21–26 — approximately 36–38 miles

**Monday:** Complete rest.

**Tuesday, 7 miles:** 6 × 200 meters at relaxed mile effort with a full 200-meter jog. Stop before generating fatigue.

**Wednesday, 6 miles:** Easy.

**Thursday, 5 miles:** 2 × 1 mile at HMP with 3 minutes easy jogging, followed by 4 relaxed strides.

## Moving the weekend long run

The assigned Saturday/Sunday placement is intentional, but it is flexible:

- When the long run moves to **Saturday**, make Friday 5–6 miles very easy and run 5–6 recovery miles Sunday.
- When it moves to **Sunday**, keep Saturday at 5–7 easy miles with optional strides.
- If Sunday’s long run becomes unexpectedly hard, remove the final two repetitions from Tuesday’s track workout.
- Never move Thursday’s tempo to Friday immediately before a Saturday long run. Skip it or replace it with 20–30 minutes steady.

## Strength and supplementary work

Because only ten weeks remain, do not begin an ambitious new lifting program. When you are already accustomed to strength training, use one or two short sessions weekly through Week 6, one session in Weeks 7–8, and no heavy lower-body lifting during the final ten days.

Place strength after Tuesday’s track session and after whichever weekend day is easy. A simple session could include split squats or step-ups, Romanian deadlifts, bent- and straight-knee calf raises, a hamstring exercise and trunk work. Keep the volume low enough that Thursday is unaffected.

## Race-specific execution

Plan to carry your own gels rather than assuming carbohydrate will be available mid-race.

For an approximately 75–85-minute effort, a practical strategy is a familiar gel shortly before the start and another around 35–45 minutes, taken with water. Practice that exact routine during Weeks 6, 8 and 9 rather than introducing it on race day.

Pacing should be patient:

1. **Miles 1–3:** Approximately 5 seconds per mile slower than goal average, especially on the early rollers. Do not bank time.
2. **Miles 3–8:** Settle at goal effort. Allow pace to drift on climbs or into a headwind.
3. **Miles 8–10:** Concentrate on posture, cadence and fueling. Begin passing rather than surging.
4. **Final 5K:** Gradually increase effort. With one mile remaining, stop protecting the target pace and race.

A sub-1:20 goal becomes a reasonable possibility if the September 6 10K is around 36:00 or faster and the Week 8 session of 3 × 2 miles around 6:05 pace remains controlled. Your old 1:18:09 is the upside of the block, not the pace that must be forced from Week 1.

After the July 23 threshold session, record the three repetition paces and average heart rates. Those numbers can be used to convert the remaining HMP and track sessions into tighter split targets.
