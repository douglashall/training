# 3 Beach Minimum to Marine Corps Marathon Training Plan

**Races:** 3 Beach Minimum Half Marathon and Marine Corps Marathon  
**Race dates:** Saturday, September 26, 2026, and Sunday, October 25, 2026  
**Plan segment:** Week 6, Monday, August 24, through race day, Sunday, October 25, 2026  
**Athlete background:** Current mileage 50–60 miles per week; historical PRs of 17:19 for 5K, 1:18:09 for the half marathon, and 2:48:50 for the marathon; recent emphasis on 100K racing; Weeks 1–5 of the half-marathon plan completed successfully.  
**Marathon goals:** A goal under 3:05, B goal under 3:10, and C goal under 3:15.  
**Weekly structure:** Monday rest, Tuesday short track speed, Thursday longer tempo or race-specific work, and a Saturday or Sunday long run.

## Overall approach

Continue the existing half-marathon plan through 3 Beach Minimum while adding two modest marathon-specific long-run upgrades. The half marathon remains the primary quality stimulus on September 26; it should not be converted into an extra-long marathon workout.

The four weeks between the races are organized as follows:

1. Recover and re-enter training during the week after the half marathon.
2. Complete one peak marathon-specific week.
3. Taper for two weeks while preserving small doses of intensity and marathon pace.
4. Arrive at Marine Corps rested, fueled, and comfortable at the selected goal pace.

Your ultra background supplies the endurance foundation, so there is no need to force a 22–24-mile run during this short bridge. The key marathon-specific sessions are the long runs on August 30, September 13, and October 11, plus the marathon-pace workouts on Thursdays after the half marathon.

## Training intensity guide

| Intensity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Recovery/easy** | RPE 2–3 out of 10. Full sentences, relaxed mechanics. Pace is secondary. |
| **Steady** | RPE 4–5. Clearly quicker than easy, but sustainable for a long time. Do not let it become another tempo. |
| **Marathon pace/MP** | Selected Marine Corps goal pace. Controlled and aerobic, especially before the half marathon. |
| **HMP** | Current target half-marathon effort, RPE 6–7. Controlled and rhythmic rather than strained. |
| **Threshold/T** | Current 50–60-minute race effort, RPE 7–8. Hard but even, with no late collapse. |
| **Fast track reps** | Current mile-to-5K effort depending on repetition length. Fast and mechanically crisp, never sprinting. |

## Marine Corps goal pace guide

| Goal | Finish time | Pace per mile | Pace per kilometer | Halfway split |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **A** | Under 3:05 | approximately 7:03 | approximately 4:23 | 1:32:30 |
| **B** | Under 3:10 | approximately 7:15 | approximately 4:30 | 1:35:00 |
| **C** | Under 3:15 | approximately 7:26 | approximately 4:37 | 1:37:30 |

For marathon-pace work before 3 Beach, use approximately **7:10–7:20 per mile**, progressing toward 7:05 only when it feels very controlled. Do not try to prove sub-3:05 fitness during the half-marathon build. Select the final marathon pace after considering the half-marathon result, recovery, and the key Week 12 long run.

## Workout conventions

All listed mileage includes warm-up and cool-down.

Before each Tuesday track session, run 2–3 easy miles, complete a few mobility drills, and run four relaxed 20-second strides. Use trainers or road flats unless you are already comfortable training in faster shoes.

For strides, accelerate smoothly for about 20 seconds and take at least 60–90 seconds of very easy recovery.

**Every Monday is complete rest from running.** Gentle walking or a few minutes of mobility is optional, but there is no required training.

## Week 6: August 24–30 — approximately 61 miles

This remains a peak-volume half-marathon week, with a controlled marathon-specific upgrade to the Sunday long run.

**Monday:** Complete rest.

**Tuesday, 10 miles:** 2 sets of 3 × 600 meters at current 5K pace, jogging 200 meters after each and 400 meters between sets. Then run 4 × 200 meters relaxed and fast with a full 200-meter jog.

**Wednesday, 9 miles:** Easy.

**Thursday, 13 miles:** 7-mile continuous progression. Begin approximately HMP plus 25–30 seconds per mile and gradually progress toward HMP. The final 1–2 miles may reach about 5 seconds faster than HMP only if you are still running smoothly. The average pace should remain slower than HMP.

**Friday, 6 miles:** Recovery.

**Saturday, 5 miles:** Easy. Skip strides unless your legs feel fresh.

**Sunday, 18 miles:** Run 9 miles easy, 3 miles at marathon pace, 1 mile easy, 3 miles at marathon pace, and 2 miles easy. Use approximately 7:10–7:20 per mile for the marathon-pace portions. Keep them relaxed and aerobic rather than turning the run into a race.

Practice your intended marathon fueling and hydration during Sunday’s run.

## Week 7: August 31–September 6 — 49–51 miles

This is a tune-up and cutback week.

**Monday:** Complete rest.

**Tuesday, 8 miles:** 8 × 200 meters at relaxed current mile effort, with a full 200-meter jog after every repetition. This is a sharpening session, not a test.

**Wednesday, 9 miles:** Easy.

**Thursday, 9 miles:** 20–25 minutes continuously at HMP, followed by 4 relaxed strides. Finish knowing you could have continued.

**Friday, 6 miles:** Recovery.

**Saturday, 6 miles:** Easy with 4 brief strides.

**Sunday, 11–13 miles total:** Run a 10K race or solo time trial. Include approximately 3 miles of warm-up, drills and strides, the 10K hard but evenly paced, and 2–4 miles of very easy cool-down. Do not extend the cool-down simply to satisfy the mileage target if your legs are depleted.

Use the result to establish your goal HMP for the final three weeks. Do not add extra mileage to turn this into a marathon long run.

## Week 8: September 7–13 — approximately 61 miles

This is the most demanding race-specific week before the half marathon.

**Monday:** Complete rest.

**Tuesday, 8 miles:** 8 × 200 meters relaxed and fast with a full 200-meter jog. Because this follows Sunday’s 10K, keep the session light.

**Wednesday, 10 miles:** Easy.

**Thursday, 13 miles:** 3 × 2 miles at goal HMP with 2 minutes easy jogging. Wear your half-marathon race shoes and use the same pre-run meal planned for race morning. All three repetitions should be within a few seconds of each other.

**Friday, 7 miles:** Recovery.

**Saturday, 5 miles:** Easy. Add 4–6 relaxed strides only when your legs feel good.

**Sunday, 18 miles:** Run 8 miles easy, 3 miles at marathon pace, 1 mile easy, 3 miles at marathon pace, and 3 miles easy. Use approximately 7:10–7:20 per mile for the marathon-pace portions.

If Thursday’s workout is unexpectedly difficult or soreness persists into the weekend, run Sunday completely easy. Practice your marathon breakfast, fueling, fluids, and shoes.

## Selecting the final marathon goal

Use the 3 Beach result as one input, not the only input. A rough race-equivalence guide is:

| Marathon goal | Approximate equivalent half marathon |
|---|---:|
| **Under 3:05** | approximately 1:28:45 |
| **Under 3:10** | approximately 1:31:10 |
| **Under 3:15** | approximately 1:33:30 |

Because Marine Corps is not a flat laboratory time trial and the marathon-specific bridge is short, look for some cushion:

- **A-goal confidence:** approximately 1:27 or faster at 3 Beach, an even or negative split, good recovery, and a smooth Week 12 twenty-miler.
- **B-goal confidence:** approximately 1:29:30 or faster, or a faster half followed by incomplete recovery.
- **C-goal confidence:** approximately 1:32 or faster with stable endurance and no significant late fade.

Weather, wind, course profile, heart rate, perceived effort, second-half execution, and recovery during the following three days all matter. Do not select a pace from finish time alone.

## Moving the weekend long run

The assigned Sunday placement is preferred for the key marathon-specific long runs because it provides more separation from Thursday’s workout.

- When a long run must move to **Saturday**, reduce Friday to 4–5 very easy miles and move Saturday’s short easy run to Sunday as recovery.
- Shorten Thursday’s quality volume by approximately one-third before a Saturday marathon-specific long run.
- If a Sunday long run becomes unexpectedly hard, reduce or remove the following Tuesday’s track repetitions.
- Never move Thursday’s quality workout to Friday immediately before a Saturday long run. Skip it or replace it with 20–30 minutes steady.
- Keep the October 11 twenty-miler on Sunday whenever practical.

## Strength and supplementary work

Continue familiar strength work, but reduce lower-body volume as the races approach. Keep upper-body work when it does not affect running.

Through September 13, use one moderate lower-body session after Tuesday and, if well tolerated, one light maintenance session after an easy day. During the half-marathon taper, avoid heavy eccentric lower-body work.

After 3 Beach, use no more than one meaningful lower-body maintenance session per week. Complete the final substantial lower-body session before October 11 and avoid heavy lower-body lifting during the final two marathon taper weeks.

## Fueling practice

Use the marathon-specific long runs on August 30, September 13, and October 11 to rehearse the complete race routine:

- Eat the same breakfast, at approximately the same interval before running, that you intend to use on marathon morning.
- Begin carbohydrate intake within the first 20–30 minutes rather than waiting for hunger or fatigue.
- Continue fueling at regular intervals throughout the run.
- Practice the carbohydrate level you already tolerate from ultra training, working toward approximately 60–90 grams per hour only when your stomach handles it comfortably.
- Rehearse fluids, electrolytes, gels or drink mix, carrying method, shoes, socks, and clothing.
- Make no new fueling or equipment experiments on race day.

## 3 Beach Minimum race-day pacing

1. **Miles 1–3:** Start approximately 5 seconds per mile slower than goal average, especially on early rollers. Do not bank time.
2. **Miles 3–8:** Settle at goal effort. Allow pace to drift on climbs, descents, or into wind rather than forcing uniform splits.
3. **Miles 8–10:** Concentrate on posture, cadence, and fueling. Begin passing rather than surging.
4. **Final 5K:** Gradually increase effort. With one mile remaining, stop protecting the target pace and race.

Recover from the half marathon before interpreting the result. The most useful indicators are the finish time, 5K splits, heart rate, how the final 5K felt, and how quickly normal mechanics return.

## Marine Corps Marathon race-day pacing

1. **Opening miles:** Run approximately 5–10 seconds per mile slower than target pace by effort. Allow congestion, turns, and early climbing to settle before locking into pace.
2. **Middle miles:** Gradually establish selected marathon pace. Stay patient, fuel on schedule, and avoid reacting to runners who surge.
3. **Miles 18–24:** Hold marathon effort rather than forcing the watch on rises or into wind. Protect fueling, posture, and cadence.
4. **Final 2.2 miles:** Begin racing when you are still mechanically sound. Expect pace to vary with terrain and focus on sustained effort through the finish.

The best goal is the fastest pace that still feels controlled in Week 12 and leaves you confident that you could continue beyond the 8-mile marathon-pace block.
